20 Healthy Snack Ideas That Are Filling and Nutritious

By Youssef EL MONTASSIR · Published 25 Nov 2026 · 7 min read

Mid-morning and mid-afternoon hunger are normal, and the snacks you choose can either steady your energy or trigger another sugar crash. This guide lists 20 healthy snack ideas that pair protein and fibre to keep you full, most of which take five minutes or less to prepare and travel well to work, university or school.

A useful snack is not a treat disguised as one: it contributes something useful, such as protein, fibre, vitamins or minerals, without relying on large amounts of added sugar, salt or refined flour.

Why choose healthy snacks during the day?

Snacking can support a balanced diet when it prevents extreme hunger that leads to heavy, ultra-processed meals later. The NHS Eatwell Guide encourages regular meals and snacks built from fruit, vegetables, beans, pulses, fish, eggs, dairy or alternatives, and unsalted nuts. The right snack also keeps blood sugar and concentration steadier through a busy morning or afternoon.

What makes a snack actually filling?

The most satisfying snacks combine at least two of the following: protein, fibre and water-rich fruit or vegetables. A sweet biscuit may taste good, but it is low in protein and fibre, so hunger returns quickly. The simple formula below works for most snacks.

20 healthy snack ideas for work, school and home

Fruit and vegetable snacks

  1. Apple slices with peanut butter. Crunchy apple for fibre and water, paired with a tablespoon of unsweetened nut butter for protein and healthy fat. Choose no-added-sugar peanut butter.
  2. Berries with plain yoghurt. Strawberries, blueberries or raspberries on plain or Greek yoghurt give vitamin C, fibre and protein in one bowl. Frozen berries work well and keep for months.
  3. Carrot and pepper sticks with hummus. Crunchy vegetables dipped in two tablespoons of hummus provide fibre plus plant protein and iron from the chickpeas.
  4. An orange or clementine. Naturally wrapped, portable and high in vitamin C and fibre. Eat the whole fruit rather than drinking juice, which loses most of the fibre.
  5. Banana with a small handful of almonds. Quick energy from the banana with protein, magnesium and healthy fats from the almonds. Ideal before or after a walk or workout.
  6. Pear with a small piece of cheese. Pears are among the higher-fibre fruits; a match of 20 to 30 g of cheese balances sweetness with protein and calcium.
  7. Cherry tomato and mozzarella bites. A mini caprese snack with cherry tomatoes, small mozzarella balls and a drizzle of olive oil and basil. Tomatoes also supply potassium and lycopene, as explained in our tomato guide.
  8. Cucumber sticks with tzatziki. Cool, hydrating vegetables with a yoghurt-based dip. It is light, low in calories and easy to pack in a small pot.

Protein-rich snacks

  1. Greek yoghurt with chia seeds. Plain Greek yoghurt provides roughly 10 to 15 g of protein per serving; a teaspoon of chia seeds adds fibre and omega-3 fats. It appears again in our healthy breakfast foods list.
  2. A small handful of unsalted nuts. About 30 g (a small handful) of almonds, walnuts or pistachios gives healthy fats, protein and magnesium. Keep the portion in mind because nuts are energy-dense, and choose unsalted versions.
  3. A boiled egg. One boiled egg delivers around 6 g of protein and choline for few calories. Batch-boil a few at the start of the week; our eggs article explains the nutrition in detail.
  4. Cottage cheese with fruit. Cottage cheese is high in protein and calcium. Pair it with pineapple, peach slices or berries, and choose lower-salt versions where available.
  5. Steamed edamame with a little salt. Young soybeans are a complete plant protein with fibre and folate. Steam and sprinkle with a pinch of salt, or buy plain frozen pods.
  6. Tuna or sardines on whole-grain crackers. Canned oily fish supplies protein and omega-3 fats. Mix a little with yoghurt rather than mayonnaise and serve on two wholegrain crackers.

Warm and pantry snacks

  1. Roasted chickpeas. Toss drained chickpeas in olive oil and smoked paprika, roast at 200 °C for 25 to 30 minutes until crisp. They replace crisps with fibre and plant protein.
  2. Avocado on whole-grain toast. Mash a quarter of an avocado on toast with lemon juice and black pepper. Add a boiled egg for a more substantial snack.
  3. Rice cakes with almond butter. Two plain rice cakes with a thin spread of nut butter give fibre and healthy fats; watch sweetened nut butters for added sugar.
  4. Plain popcorn. Air-popped or stovetop popcorn without butter and sugar is a high-fibre, low-calorie snack. It fits the whole-grain variety encouraged in the 50 healthy foods list.
  5. Green smoothie. Blend banana, frozen berries, plain yoghurt, a handful of spinach and a spoon of seeds. The flavour of fruit masks the leaves, and you get fibre, protein and vegetables in one glass.
  6. Homemade unsalted trail mix. Combine unsalted nuts, seeds, a little dried fruit without added sugar and a few plain whole-grain cereal pieces. Portion into small bags to avoid overeating.

Practical tips for healthy snacking

  • Plan ahead: pack one snack for mid-morning and one for the afternoon so you do not rely on vending machines.
  • Watch portions: nuts, nut butters and dried foods are calorie-dense; use small pots or bags rather than eating from the pack.
  • Read labels: check for added sugar in yoghurts, cereal bars and drinks, and for salt in canned and packet snacks.
  • Pair, do not isolate: combine a protein or fat food with a fruit, vegetable or wholegrain whenever you can.
  • Hydrate first: thirst can feel like hunger; drink water before deciding on a snack.
  • Keep treats occasional: snacks such as chocolate or crisps are fine now and then; the everyday choices matter most.

Final Thoughts

Healthy snacks do not need to be complicated or expensive. Build most of them from fruit, vegetables, plain dairy or alternatives, beans, eggs, nuts and whole grains, pair protein with fibre, and plan a few options for the week. Frequently asked questions

What are the healthiest snacks for weight management?

Snacks that combine protein and fibre, such as yoghurt with berries, hummus with vegetables, a boiled egg with fruit, or nuts with an apple, tend to be filling for relatively few calories. Portion size still matters, especially for nuts and dried fruit.

Which snacks are easy to keep at work without refrigeration?

Fresh fruit, unsalted nuts and seeds, rice cakes with nut butter, wholegrain crackers with canned tuna, dried fruit without added sugar, and plain popcorn all travel well. Keep a small jar of nut butter and some fruit at your desk.

Are nuts fattening?

Nuts are energy-dense, so large unrestricted portions add calories, but a daily small handful is associated with good diets in research. Choose unsalted varieties and keep to about 30 g per serving.

Is fruit enough as a snack?

Fruit gives fibre, vitamins and natural sweetness, but adding a little protein such as yoghurt, nuts, cheese or nut butter makes the snack more satisfying and steady between meals.
