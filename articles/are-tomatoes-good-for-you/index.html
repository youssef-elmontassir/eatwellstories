Are Tomatoes Good for You? Benefits, Nutrition and Ways to Enjoy Them

By Youssef EL MONTASSIR · Published 25 Nov 2026 · 7 min read

Are tomatoes good for you? Understanding their health benefits

Yes, for most people tomatoes are a healthy food. A medium raw tomato has only about 22 calories, plus a useful amount of potassium and vitamin C, and it is made mostly of water.

Tomatoes are also the main dietary source of lycopene, the pigment that makes them red.

Scientists have not shown that lycopene or any other single antioxidant directly prevents cancer, so this article describes benefits as potential contributions to a healthy diet and not as cures or guarantees.

How tomato health benefits support a balanced diet

Tomato health benefits are strongest when tomatoes are part of an overall pattern of eating, such as a Mediterranean-style diet rich in vegetables, fruit, whole grains, legumes and olive oil. No single food can make a diet healthy by itself.

Tomatoes fit easily into that pattern because they are:

  • Versatile, so they work in salads, sauces, soups, stews, sandwiches and snacks.
  • Affordable and available in many forms, including fresh, canned, passata, paste and
  • sun-dried.

If you take medication or follow a special diet, such as a low-potassium diet for kidney disease, ask your doctor or dietitian how much tomato is right for you.

Calories and nutritional value of tomatoes

Tomatoes are a low-calorie food. A small tomato has about 16 kcal, a medium one about 22 kcal, and a large one about 33 kcal. A cup of cherry tomatoes has about 27 kcal. Most of the energy comes from natural sugars and a little fibre, so tomatoes add volume and flavour to meals without many calories.

Processed tomato products can look different on the label. Tomato juice, sauce and soup often contain added salt, so check the sodium content and choose reduced-salt versions when you can.

7 Potential Tomato Health Benefits

These benefits describe how the nutrients in tomatoes may contribute to health as part of a balanced diet. Tomatoes do not prevent or treat any disease.

Support overall heart health

Tomatoes provide potassium, fibre and vitamin C, which all play a part in a heart-healthy diet. Potassium helps maintain normal blood pressure, especially when sodium intake is moderate. Studies also link diets rich in lycopene with a lower risk of heart disease, but many of these are observational, so they cannot prove that tomatoes are the cause.

Help maintain normal vision

Tomatoes contain beta-carotene, which the body converts to vitamin A. Vitamin A is needed for normal vision, especially in low light, and for healthy eye surfaces. Tomatoes are one of many foods, along with carrots, leafy greens and eggs, that contribute to your vitamin A intake.

Support digestive health

Tomatoes contain fibre and a lot of water, which both help keep digestion regular. A medium tomato has about 1.5 g of fibre. Eating the skin and pulp, rather than only drinking the juice, keeps more of that fibre. Tomatoes are also acidic, so some people with acid reflux find that they trigger symptoms.

Help support a balanced diet

Tomatoes add flavour, colour and nutrients to meals while staying low in calories, fat and sodium (when eaten fresh or in reduced-salt products). That makes it easier to eat more vegetables, which is one of the most consistent messages in healthy eating guidance from WHO and Harvard.

Raw vs. Cooked Tomatoes: Which Is Better?

Neither is better overall: each gives you something different, so eating both is a good plan.

Best usesSalads, sandwiches, snacksSauces, soups, stews, roasting In a Cornell University study, cooking tomatoes reduced vitamin C by about 10 to 29 percent but increased the amount of lycopene the body can use, along with overall antioxidant activity. Human trials have also found that lycopene is absorbed better from tomato paste or purée than from fresh tomatoes. Lycopene is fat-soluble, so a little healthy fat helps. In one small trial, people who ate diced tomatoes cooked with olive oil had a clear rise in blood lycopene, while the group that cooked them without oil did not. Drizzle olive oil over tomatoes, or pair them with avocado, nuts or cheese. The practical takeaway: eat raw tomatoes for vitamin C and freshness, and cooked, canned or puréed tomatoes for easier lycopene absorption.

Final Thoughts

Tomato health benefits come from a simple combination: few calories, a good amount of potassium and vitamin C, and lycopene that is easier to absorb when tomatoes are cooked with a little olive oil. They are a useful part of a balanced diet, but not a cure or a shortcut. Eat them raw and cooked, in fresh, canned and roasted forms, and alongside plenty of other vegetables, whole grains and legumes.

Frequently asked questions

Is it healthy to eat tomatoes every day?

For most people, yes. Tomatoes are low in calories and provide vitamin C, potassium and lycopene, and they count towards the 400 g of fruit and vegetables per day that WHO recommends for adults. Variety still matters, so eat tomatoes alongside other vegetables. Some people with acid reflux find that tomatoes trigger symptoms, and people on potassium-restricting diets should check with their doctor.

Are raw tomatoes healthier than cooked tomatoes?

Not necessarily. Raw tomatoes keep more vitamin C, while cooked tomatoes release more usable lycopene. Eating both gives you the benefits of each, and adding a little olive oil helps the body absorb lycopene.

How many tomatoes can you eat in a day?

There is no official daily limit for tomatoes. A common portion is one medium tomato or about a cup of chopped or cherry tomatoes, and two or three portions across the day is reasonable for most people. Eating very large amounts may cause heartburn or stomach upset in sensitive people, and a rare tomato allergy can cause symptoms such as an itchy mouth.

Are tomatoes good for digestion?

Tomatoes contain fibre and water, which both support regular digestion, and a medium tomato provides about 1.5 g of fibre. Leaving the skin on keeps more fibre. If tomatoes worsen heartburn or reflux for you, try cooked tomatoes in smaller amounts or talk to your doctor.
